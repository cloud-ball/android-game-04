# Art direction of visually praised mini-golf games: techniques an ORIGINAL arcade mini-golf game can use

Researched 2026-11-15. Fifteen web searches and fetches. Commercial titles are named only as sources of evidence. Every guidance item under "Inferences" is described as a generic technique, not as a copy of a specific game's look. Where a game's look is distinctive enough to be a copying risk, it is flagged.

Source quality note: the most authoritative material on mini-golf art direction is first-person writing and interviews from Mighty Coconut (Walkabout Mini Golf, VR). Its senior art director is a former theme-park designer. Mobile battler titles (Golf Battle, Mini Golf King, Golf Blitz) get little visual critique from major outlets. Much of what turns up for them is store copy or AI-written aggregator pages, which were treated as low reliability and mostly excluded.

---

## 1. Which mini-golf / arcade golf games are praised (or criticised) for visuals, and what traits do reviews call out?

### Takeaway
Praise goes to games whose courses feel like coherent, themed *places* with a strong opening vista and layered detail (Walkabout Mini Golf), or games with whimsical, animated transformations of the course (the original Wonderputt). Criticism falls on "crisp but bland/generic" looks (Golf Battle, Golf With Your Friends), courses with no landmarks that blur together (Pro Putt), and pastel sameness ("pastel slop", Wonderputt Forever). A simple, low-poly, bright style is fine with reviewers, even praised, as long as it reads clearly and runs smoothly.

### Inferences
- The pattern that draws praise is **"place, not playfield"**: courses that read as somewhere specific (a studio lot, an underwater city, a seasonal hillside), each with a landmark. The pattern that draws criticism is a generic green felt course floating in a generic setting, even when it is clean and colourful.
- Low-poly or simple geometry is **not** penalised. Reviewers accept and even praise simple stylisation if it is coherent, smooth (60 fps comes up repeatedly) and readable. Fairway Flick does not need high-fidelity assets. It needs strong theming, landmarks and animation.
- **Pastel-everything is a risk.** If every theme uses the same soft, low-contrast palette, holes blur together. Each theme needs its own dominant hue *and* value structure.
- Copying-risk flags (do NOT imitate):
  - Walkabout's specific course themes and licensed tie-ins (Myst, Labyrinth, Meow Wolf, Jules Verne), its art-deco hole borders, and its cartoon character/animal designs.
  - Wonderputt's signature single-screen island that morphs hole to hole.
  - Golf Peaks' card-driven isometric tile presentation.
  - What the Golf's flat minimalist gag style.
  - The Miniclip battler UI layout (top-bar player avatars, chest/reward screens).
  - Fairway Flick should use its own theme set and its own rail and cup "shape language".

- No major-outlet visual critique was found for the big mobile battlers (Golf Battle, Mini Golf King, Golf Rival). The Mini Golf King "semi-realistic" description came only from an AI aggregator and was excluded. A screenshot audit of current store listings would fill this gap, but it was not possible in this text-only session.
- No Apple Arcade *mini*-golf title with strong visual reviews was confirmed. Golf Peaks and What the Golf appear on multiple platforms. Wonderputt Forever is on Netflix, not Apple Arcade.

## 2. Art-direction recipes: stylisation level, palettes and value structure, themed worlds, landmarks, animated elements

### Takeaway
The best-regarded approach is theme-park thinking. Each course is a themed "land" with an establishing vista, a consistent internal rule set (shape language, light direction), and distinct "chapter" scenes. Stylisation is simple or low-poly cartoon with baked lighting. Readability comes from **value** separation (light vs dark), not from hue, and should be checked with grayscale screenshots.

### Cited Findings
- Walkabout's art director (Don Carson, a former Imagineer) writes: "By using lighting, color, and composition, we draw attention to the most important elements." — [UploadVR guest article, Carson, 2 Feb 2025](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Carson: "Starting with breathtaking vistas creates moments of wonder." Establishing shots "set the tone and align with what players expect when they step into a new course." Environments should answer "Where am I?" — [same](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Carson: "By distilling design into clear, simple rules and adhering to them, we preserve the believability of our worlds." Breaking those rules "risks pulling players out of the immersive experience." — [same](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Carson: "Like chapters in a book, we create distinct moments within our courses." Small touches like "an unexpected flourish, a hidden clue" leave lasting impressions. — [same](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- For Passport: Hollywood, the team designed a "shape language" for gameplay and visuals, and hole borders got a period-specific look after many iterations. Places were designed first, and holes were then fitted into them. The entry gate is an "establishing shot." — [UploadVR, Mike Johnson, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.uploadvr.com/walkabout-mini-golf-passport-hollywood-course/)
- Upside Town (gravity course): sunlight is cast at a consistent angle across buildings so inverted geometry still reads as one coherent space. The course runs as a continuous route that ends on a high vantage point overlooking the city. — [UploadVR, Ian Hamilton, 9 Mar 2023](https://uploadvr.com/upside-town-walkabout-course-design/)
- A typical Walkabout course takes months, versus years for a theme-park attraction. Its theme-park design pedigree is explicit. — [Meta developer blog, Carson interview](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/walkabout-mini-golf-don-carson-interview-20000-leagues/)
- Value-structure technique: in DOTA 2, the ground is darker grays than the units, and units are the brightest, highest-contrast elements. The ground's internal value range is kept small. The fix for a muddy scene was to "darken the background, brighten the walkable surfaces, and boost the character." "Contrast draws attention, and if everything is drawing attention, nothing will." — [Game Developer, Peter Angstadt, 12 Mar 2015](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)
- Most readability problems are value problems, not hue problems. A detailed background in the same mid-tones as gameplay elements swallows them. "Grayscale screenshots are the cheap audit." Detail is a budget: if everything is detailed, there is no hierarchy. — [bugnet.io blog](https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail)
- A talk recap on stylised art (Arranger) describes moving toward abstraction so that "gameplay elements take priority while depicting a larger world around the player." — [Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/from-2-5d-to-clarity-creating-the-art-of-arranger/)
- Themed worlds found in praised titles: seasonal (summer, autumn, winter, in Cloudlands); beach, desert and forest (Pro Putt); snow melting into a lily pond (Wonderputt); underwater, mythological, film studio and city/gravity (Walkabout). — [Cloudlands, GameBrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/cloudlands-vr-minigolf); [6DOF Reviews](https://www.6dofreviews.com/reviews/top-golf-with-pro-putt-review); [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/wonderputt/review/); [Meta blog, Mount Olympus](https://www.meta.com/blog/walkabout-mini-golf-mount-olympus-course-launch/)
- An artist building props and murals for a real jungle-themed mini-golf course changed the murals to match each hole's content, building a story across the course. — [ArtStation, Victoria March, "Jungle Themed Golf"](https://victoriamarch.artstation.com/projects/9ed1Zq)

### Inferences (actionable, generic techniques)
- **Stylisation level:** use smooth, simple toon or low-poly forms. Prefer chunky, bevelled shapes with flat or gradient-ramp shading over textured realism. This suits a mobile GPU budget and matches what reviewers accept. Use 1 to 3 colour ramps per material instead of detailed albedo maps.
- **Value structure (the most important rule):** assign a fixed value order and verify it with a grayscale screenshot of every hole.
  - Darkest: off-course surroundings (water, deep foliage, void, or a shadowed cliff face). Keep their internal value range narrow.
  - Mid-dark: the playing carpet. Use a saturated mid-value hue with low internal contrast, maybe a subtle stripe or mow pattern within a few % of value.
  - Light: rails/borders. A bright edge clearly outlines the playable shape.
  - Brightest/most saturated: ball, cup ring/flag, aim guide, interactive hazards and boosters.
  - Fairway Flick could pick the reverse order, but whichever order it picks must stay consistent within a theme.
- **Palette per theme:** give each world one dominant hue family, one complementary accent reserved for interactive objects, and a sky that controls overall mood. Change carpet hue between worlds (green / teal / terracotta / violet) so worlds are recognisable at a glance. This avoids the "pastel slop" sameness.
- **Landmarks:** one large, silhouette-readable landmark per course (or per 3 holes), visible from the overview/map camera. Place it off the playfield so it frames but never occludes. Examples: a lighthouse, a giant teapot, a crashed rocket, a ruined arch, a volcano.
- **Establishing shot:** start each hole or course with a 1 to 2 s camera fly-in that shows the landmark and the cup, then settle to the play camera. This is Carson's "Where am I?" moment, and it fits Fairway Flick's existing map view.
- **Consistent rules:** use one sun direction per world, one rail profile per world, and one colour meaning per mechanic across all worlds.
- **Animated elements:** ambient animation outside the playfield (flags fluttering, water UV scroll, swaying foliage via vertex shader, rotating windmill-like contraptions, birds, clouds) makes a scene feel alive cheaply. Gameplay-relevant animation (moving obstacles) needs a strong colour code and a predictable rhythm.
- **Transformation moments:** brief whimsical transitions between holes (scenery morph, props popping up) were the single most praised element of the original Wonderputt. A generic version is fine (a hole "builds itself" by tiles popping in). Do not copy the island-morph concept wholesale.

### Gaps
- No primary source gave specific hex palettes or value numbers for a commercial mini-golf game.
- No GDC Vault talk specifically about mini-golf art direction was found. The value-structure guidance comes from general game-art articles.

## 3. Set dressing: density, prop types, keeping the playfield readable while surroundings are rich

### Takeaway
Rich surroundings come from layered, story-bearing vignettes placed *around* the holes. The holes are fitted into a designed place, and props are culled or trimmed for performance. Detail must be budgeted: dense and lower-contrast at the edges, sparse and clean on the carpet.

### Cited Findings
- Carson: "Layers of design enrich the environment, making it more vibrant and rewarding for players who take the time to explore." "We tell stories through small, evocative moments scattered throughout the environment." Stories should stay "broad and intuitive." — [UploadVR, Carson 2025](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Hollywood course: studio-life vignettes such as lot cats, the commissary, and peeks into soundstages. Props were reused from past courses. Furniture and room layouts were adjusted to fit the holes, not the reverse. — [UploadVR, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.uploadvr.com/walkabout-mini-golf-passport-hollywood-course/)
- Performance trade-offs: "we're constantly pushing the limits as to how many polygons we can get squeezed into an environment." Props inside buildings aren't rendered once the player leaves: "you don't want to have to be rendering them all the time." Shadows are baked at the end ("the final bake where the shadows are all baked in"). — [same](https://www.uploadvr.com/walkabout-mini-golf-passport-hollywood-course/)
- Cloudlands courses were praised as "fantastical without being overwhelming." — [GameBrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/cloudlands-vr-minigolf)
- Pro Putt was criticised for courses lacking distinctive landmarks; flora/fauna details alone didn't make them memorable. — [6DOF Reviews](https://www.6dofreviews.com/reviews/top-golf-with-pro-putt-review)
- In a stylised scene, a detailed background in the same mid-tones as gameplay swallows the gameplay. Detail is a budget. — [bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail)

### Inferences (actionable)
- **Three-ring density model:**
  1. **Ring 0, the carpet:** almost nothing but gameplay objects. No decorative props, decals or noisy textures on the putting surface.
  2. **Ring 1, a 0.5 to 2 m apron outside the rails:** low, simple props that frame the hole edge (planters, stones, small lamps, flower beds, theme kit pieces). Medium saturation, kept below rail height or set back, so they never occlude the ball from the play camera.
  3. **Ring 2, the backdrop:** big shapes, the landmark, and dense clustered props (trees, buildings, cliffs). Higher density but lower contrast. Use atmospheric/height fog or value compression toward the sky colour.
- **Vignettes:** 2 to 4 tiny story moments per hole in Ring 1/2, e.g. a picnic blanket with an ant trail, a tipped-over bucket, a sleeping cat on a crate. These reward the map view without touching readability.
- **Portrait framing:** a portrait phone with a high camera shows mostly carpet and Ring 1. Ring 2 appears mainly in the top third of the screen, so put the landmark and sky interest there.
- **Mobile budget:**
  - Use MultiMesh/instancing for repeated foliage and props.
  - Bake lighting or use a single directional light with no realtime shadows on Ring 2.
  - Cull Ring 2 detail per hole: only the current hole's surroundings are loaded (matching Walkabout's cull-when-not-visible approach).
  - Reuse a themed kit across holes.
- **Places first:** design the themed place (garden, harbour, ruins), then fit holes into it. Avoid a generic tile course plopped on a plane. Fairway Flick's tile-map baking can still work if each world has a backdrop scene the tiles sit inside.

### Gaps
- No quantitative density figures (props per metre, polygon budgets) were found for any mobile mini-golf game.

## 4. Course object design: rails, cups, ramps/tunnels/loops, obstacle visual language

### Takeaway
There is little direct critique of rail and cup styling. The strongest evidence is that the hole-border profile is a deliberate, iterated part of each world's "shape language," and that reviewers list ramps, bridges, banked turns, ice, branching paths and moving set pieces as visual and gameplay variety. Colour-coded obstacle language is a widely used convention, but no mini-golf-specific source documents it.

### Cited Findings
- Walkabout's Hollywood course: hole borders were given a period look that "took many iterations to get right," as part of a combined gameplay and visual "shape language." — [UploadVR, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.uploadvr.com/walkabout-mini-golf-passport-hollywood-course/)
- Golf Battle's later courses add branching pathways, precarious bridges, banked turns and slippery ice. — [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/games/golf-battle-fast-paced-online-mini-golf/)
- Golf Battle was noted for realistic courses with sand traps but no windmills or cartoon hazards. A reviewer framed the absence of classic whimsical obstacles as defining its (more generic) character. — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/golf-battle)
- Walkabout's Myst course uses triggers you hit with the ball to change the course itself. Interactive cause-and-effect objects are a feature. — [Meta blog, Myst launch](https://www.meta.com/blog/walkabout-mini-golf-myst-launch-meta-quest-2/)
- Carson: "Funny interactions and playful cause-and-effect moments let players share in the joke." — [UploadVR, Carson 2025](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Older mobile titles were praised for loop-the-loops and sand traps as visual set pieces. — [Windows Central, Mini Golf Stars](https://www.windowscentral.com/mini-golf-stars-windows-phone-game-review)

### Inferences (actionable)
- **Rails:** one profile per world, readable as a continuous light-value outline.
  - Garden: rounded painted wood with a darker cap.
  - Ruins: chunky stone blocks with bevels.
  - Beach: rope-and-post or driftwood.
  - Candy/toy: glossy tube.
  - Space: glowing strip on a dark rail.
  - Keep the top face lighter than the carpet and the inner face slightly darker, so the edge reads even from directly above. Bevel the top edge so it catches the sun light (a cheap specular highlight).
- **Bumpers/bouncy rails:** a different material and colour from normal rails (e.g. saturated rubber with a squash animation and a flash on hit), so players learn "this rail returns more energy."
- **Cup:** a dark hole (darkest value on the playfield) with a bright contrasting rim ring and a tall flag. The flag stays visible above occluders at all camera heights. An optional soft pulsing glow ring or vertical light shaft shows the cup in the overview. Use a celebratory particle burst and flag animation on sink.
- **Ramps/jumps:** add chevron or stripe markings on ramp faces in the "boost" accent colour, plus a lip highlight so the crest reads. Paint the landing zone with a subtle target marking.
- **Tunnels/pipes:** colour-matched entrance and exit rims (pair A = orange, pair B = blue) so players can predict the connection. Use a dark interior with an exit glint.
- **Suggested colour language** (convention-based, not sourced). Keep these hues out of the decorative palette:
  - **Red/orange stripes:** danger or out-of-bounds (water edge, pits).
  - **Yellow/amber:** boost or speed pads.
  - **Cyan/blue:** ice or slippery surfaces.
  - **Brown/tan grain:** sand or slow surfaces.
  - **Green:** the normal carpet.
  - **Purple/magenta:** teleport or magic.
- **Moving obstacles:** a strong silhouette and a predictable visible rhythm (e.g. a light that blinks before a gate closes). Animate them on the fixed physics tick for determinism, consistent with the project's rules.

### Gaps
- No reviewer or developer source specifically discusses cup or flag styling, glowing cup rings, or danger=red / boost=yellow conventions in mini-golf. Those recommendations are inferences from general game-design convention.

## 5. Lighting mood per theme and sky treatments

### Takeaway
Developer sources stress lighting as the main tool for directing attention, a consistent sun direction for coherence, and baked shadows for performance. Specific per-theme lighting moods (golden hour, night) are not documented in the sources found. They are offered here as inferred technique.

### Cited Findings
- "By using lighting, color, and composition, we draw attention to the most important elements." — [UploadVR, Carson 2025](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Consistent sunlight angle keeps a complex (inverted-gravity) course readable as one space. — [UploadVR, Upside Town, 2023](https://uploadvr.com/upside-town-walkabout-course-design/)
- Walkabout bakes its final shadows late in production. Lighting is a dedicated team pass, with the model passed "between their lighting, sound, animation, and gameplay teams." — [UploadVR Hollywood 2026](https://www.uploadvr.com/walkabout-mini-golf-passport-hollywood-course/); [UploadVR, Carson 2025](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)
- Cloudlands themes holes by season. Seasonal lighting and colour is an accepted variety lever. — [GameBrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/cloudlands-vr-minigolf)

### Inferences (actionable, Godot 4 / mobile)
- **One directional sun per world**, angled 35 to 50 degrees so rails cast short readable shadows on the carpet. The shadows help depth perception of rails and ramps from a high camera.
- **Mood presets per world:**
  - **Sunny midday garden:** neutral-warm sun, light blue gradient sky, high ambient.
  - **Golden-hour beach/harbour:** low warm sun, peach-to-lavender sky gradient, longer shadows, warm rim on rails.
  - **Overcast/misty ruins:** soft cool light, stronger fog, saturated moss accents.
  - **Night/neon (space, carnival):** dark sky with stars, emissive rails and cup ring, the ball emissive or with a trail.
  - Night worlds rely on emissive materials and glow rather than many realtime lights.
- **Sky:** a gradient or procedural sky with stylised painted clouds, not an HDRI photo. Height fog fades Ring 2 toward the sky colour (value compression = readability). Sky hue sets mood, but the carpet keeps its value slot.
- **Mobile constraints:**
  - Prefer one shadowed directional light, with baked AO or lightmaps for static course geometry.
  - Use light glow/bloom sparingly (it helps cup and ball pop at night).
  - Avoid SSAO/SSR on the Mobile renderer.
  - Fake contact shadows under the ball with a blob decal or a small dark disc. This also aids readability.

### Gaps
- No sources discussed per-theme lighting in specific mini-golf titles, or sky treatments. All mood presets above are inferences.

## 6. Readability: ball visibility, aim line, cup highlight, contrast against carpet

### Takeaway
Reviewers mainly mention readability indirectly. Simple and vibrant styles are praised for "favouring readability over realism," and slingshot press-drag-release is the standard mobile aiming input. General readability research says the interactive objects should be the highest-contrast, brightest elements on screen against a narrow-value ground. Grayscale checks are the standard audit.

### Cited Findings
- Super Battle Golf's style is described as favouring readability over realism and being "easy on both the eyes and the graphics card." — [GameBrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/super-battle-golf); [Gaming Age 2026](https://gaming-age.com/?p=113354)
- Mobile aim standard: "press and drag on that to aim your shot, and then release to let fly" (Golf Blitz). — [Pocket Gamer Golf Blitz review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/golf-blitz/review/)
- Ground should use a narrow value range darker than the gameplay units, and units should be brightest and highest-contrast. Overusing contrast creates confusion. — [Game Developer, Angstadt 2015](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)
- Readability failures are mainly value failures. Use grayscale screenshots. Stress-test in the busiest moment, not a calm scene. — [bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/readable-game-art-why-clarity-beats-detail)
- A Mini Golf King player comment notes a guide line on the fairway, and that raised spots can send the ball off the predicted line. Players do notice when the guide disagrees with the terrain. — [GameBrain, Mini Golf King](https://gamebrain.co/game/mini-golf-king) (user comment; low weight)

### Inferences (actionable)
- **Ball:**
  - Pure white or near-white with a thin dark outline or rim shading. An inverted-hull or fresnel rim works cheaply in Godot.
  - Add a soft blob contact shadow to anchor it on the carpet and ramps.
  - Add a short fading trail when the ball moves fast (a ribbon or GPU particles; avoid per-frame allocations). Fade it out at rest.
  - On very light carpets (snow, sand worlds), switch to a saturated ball colour or a stronger outline.
  - Optionally show an off-screen or occlusion indicator (silhouette through walls via a depth-test-disabled outline pass) when rails or props hide the ball.
- **Aim/slingshot guide:**
  - Use dotted or dashed segments that taper and fade with distance, in a colour that is not used by any hazard (white with a coloured core works on every carpet).
  - Predict only the first leg plus a short first-bounce stub. A full trajectory removes skill and, as the Mini Golf King comment shows, invites "the line lied" complaints on bumpy terrain.
  - Show power through line length/colour and the pull-back band. Don't use a separate meter that pulls the eye away.
  - Draw the guide slightly above the carpet with depth test off, so ramps never clip it.
- **Cup:**
  - Darkest spot on the carpet, with a bright rim ring and a tall flag in a world-constant accent colour.
  - In map or overview, a subtle pulsing ring or light beam makes it findable in under 1 s.
  - Add a proximity effect (rim glows brighter as the ball approaches) to aid near-cup reads.
- **Carpet:**
  - Saturated but mid-value, with a very low-contrast surface pattern (mow stripes ≤ 5 to 8 % value difference) so speed and direction read without noise.
  - Slopes can be shown by subtle shading or gradient arrows. Keep them in the carpet's value band.
- **Audit loop:** for every new world or hole, capture the shot screenshot (project `--shot`), desaturate it, and check value order and the ball/cup/aim pop. Check at phone size, not on a monitor.

### Gaps
- No source documented how specific mobile mini-golf games style their aim lines, ball trails or cup highlights. App-store screenshot analysis was not possible in this session.
- No accessibility-specific (colour-blind) guidance for mini-golf was found. Pairing colour codes with shape or pattern (chevrons for boost, stripes for danger) is recommended as an inference.

## 7. Synthesis: an actionable recipe for Fairway Flick (ORIGINAL; Godot 4.7, Android portrait)

### Takeaway
Premium-feeling mini-golf comes from coherent themed places with a landmark and an establishing shot, a strict value hierarchy that makes ball, cup and aim the brightest things on screen, a consistent per-world shape language for rails and props, cheap ambient animation, and celebratory micro-moments. It does not come from polygon count. Reviewers accept simple low-poly art and punish generic sameness.

### Cited Findings
- Simple low-poly style works when physics, coherence and smoothness are strong. — [Gaming Age, Walkabout](https://gaming-age.com/?p=86544); [Nintendo Life, 60 fps note](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/what_the_golf)
- Generic-but-crisp is judged "bland" and "not anything special." — [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/games/golf-battle-fast-paced-online-mini-golf/); [Gaming.net](https://www.gaming.net/reviews/golf-with-your-friends-review-xbox-playstation-switch-pc)
- Lack of landmarks makes courses feel the same. — [6DOF Reviews](https://www.6dofreviews.com/reviews/top-golf-with-pro-putt-review)
- Whimsical transitions and animation drive the "breathtaking" praise. — [Pocket Gamer, Wonderputt 2012](https://www.pocketgamer.com/wonderputt/review/)
- The theme-park principles (vista, rules, chapters, layered detail, humour) come from the genre's most praised art director. — [UploadVR, Carson 2025](https://www.uploadvr.com/principles-of-course-design-in-walkabout-mini-golf/)

### Inferences (prioritised checklist)
1. **Lock a value ladder and a colour-meaning table** project-wide (surroundings < carpet < rails < interactive/ball/cup/aim). Add a grayscale screenshot check to the definition of done for visual tasks.
2. **Per-world kit:**
   - carpet hue
   - rail profile and material
   - a 6 to 10 prop set for Ring 1
   - backdrop pieces for Ring 2
   - one landmark
   - a sky gradient and fog colour
   - a sun angle and colour
   - Suggested original themes:
     - backyard garden at midday
     - harbour at golden hour
     - mossy ruins in mist
     - toy-box / candy workshop
     - night carnival or moon base with emissive rails
3. **Establishing fly-in** to the landmark and cup at hole start (reuse the existing map view), then a steady high play camera. Keep the landmark in the top third of the portrait frame.
4. **Ambient animation pass:** water UV scroll, foliage vertex sway, flags, one big animated contraption per world off the carpet. Gameplay-moving objects get colour coding and a telegraph.
5. **Juice:** cup-sink burst, flag wave, rim glow, ball trail at speed, bumper squash and flash. Optionally, a hole "assembles" itself at load as a signature transition (generic tile pop-in, not a copy of any island-morph concept).
6. **Mobile budget:**
   - one shadowed directional light
   - baked AO for static tiles
   - MultiMesh for scatter
   - per-hole Ring 2 culling
   - glow only for emissive accents
   - no per-frame allocations in trails or guides
7. **Avoid copying:**
   - No art-deco border motif or licensed-style themes like Walkabout's.
   - No single-island-morph presentation.
   - No card-tile isometric look.
   - No battler HUD layout.
   - Keep display names and visual identity original per CLAUDE.md.
