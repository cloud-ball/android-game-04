# Build Fairway Flick on one deterministic solver

Fairway Flick should run one custom, fixed-step, pure-GDScript ball solver from the moment the ball is struck until it stops: RK4 flight, a Penner-style bounce, a Stimpmeter-calibrated roll and an analytic cup. Nothing should be handed to Jolt. This is the main way the plan in `docs/games/golf.md` and `.claude/rules/golf.md` should change. Two other assumptions need replacing too. The aero coefficients in those files ("Cd 0.27 -> 0.32 above S = 0.1, Cl = 0.54·S^0.4 clamped at 0.25") made drives about 12% short in the research team's RK4 check, and irons flew too flat. A smooth, literature-based Cd/Cl pair costs the same and lands within about 3-7% of TrackMan tour carries. The 4%/s spin decay can stay. The Jolt RigidBody3D landing hand-off would throw away the determinism that CPU rollouts, replays and golden-hash tests need. The project already has evidence that a custom solver works: `shared/cue_sim` is bit-exact between desktop and an Adreno 642L phone. On the design side, the evidence favours a single-thumb pull-back-and-release swing where power comes from pull distance, not flick speed. It also favours a draggable landing marker, an animated slope grid on greens, nine executive-length holes (par 31-32) baked from tiles into a heightfield, and a CPU that picks shots by Monte Carlo expected strokes. Each CPU tier's error should be anchored to Broadie's measured amateur and pro dispersion. The report ends with a defaults table, a table of every assumption to change, and a ten-step build order whose first deliverable is a failing carry-distance test.

## The planned aero model makes drives 12% short and irons fly flat

### Every full shot lands inside the drag crisis, so Cd must depend on Reynolds number

Wind-tunnel data on 13 production balls shows **Cd of about 0.5 below Re ≈ 5×10⁴ and about 0.2 above Re ≈ 10⁵** ([Lyu et al. 2018](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/595d/3d1f91e240cb47e153b8d0ef586b8caa67c4.pdf)). At sea-level air, Re ≈ 2,890 × V (V in m/s), so the transition falls between about 18 and 35 m/s. That matters because every full shot passes through it. A tour drive leaves the club at 76 m/s (Re ≈ 2.2×10⁵). Penner's model, and the team's re-run of the open-source flight model described below, both land every full shot at about **21-27 m/s** ([Penner 2002](http://waddengolfacademy.com/putting/Penner_The%20Run%20of%20a%20Golf%20Ball.pdf)), which is Re ≈ 5.5-7.2×10⁴, right on the steep part of the curve. A constant Cd of 0.27 is too high at launch and too low at landing. The planned step (0.27, rising to 0.32 once S passes 0.1) adds a drag jump that does not exist. Real data shows a smooth rise with spin ([Kensrud 2010](https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/kensrudthesis.pdf)). The cost of getting this wrong is large: a Cd difference of only 0.02 between balls moved carry by **18 m** ([Lyu et al. 2018](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/595d/3d1f91e240cb47e153b8d0ef586b8caa67c4.pdf)).

Two open-source flight models handle this the same way. One uses a logistic blend from 0.5 down to 0.225, centred on Re = 80,000, plus a spin term of 0.18·(1 − e^(−3S²)) ([opengolfcoach](https://docs.rs/crate/opengolfcoach/0.3.0/source/src/trajectory.rs)). The other uses a piecewise-linear version, 0.5 + kS below Re = 5×10⁴, blending to 0.2 + kS above 10⁵ ([libgolf](https://gdifiore.github.io/libgolf/docs/aerodynamic_model.html)). Fairway Flick should use the piecewise-linear form, because it has no `exp()` in the hot loop and is bit-identical across platforms.

### The lift clamp at 0.25 is active for most of every iron shot

Spin ratio S = ωr/V is about 0.07 for a tour drive at launch, 0.29 for a 7-iron and 0.45 for a pitching wedge, computed from TrackMan's 2023 tour launch data ([Top Shelf Golf / TrackMan](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/golf-swing-speed-chart)). A Cl clamp of 0.25 therefore binds for most of every iron and wedge flight. Measured lift keeps rising with spin past S = 0.25 ([Kensrud 2010](https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/kensrudthesis.pdf)). The quadratic fit to the 13-ball average, **Cl = 1.99S − 3.255S² up to S = 0.25 and then linear with slope 0.3625**, reproduces that ([opengolfcoach](https://docs.rs/crate/opengolfcoach/0.3.0/source/src/trajectory.rs)). The "0.54·S^0.4" law could not be traced to a primary source. Patent excerpts that quote Smits & Smith are OCR-garbled ([US 11230375](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11230375)). The power law also needs `pow()`, which is the transcendental most likely to differ between Android's bionic libm and desktop.

The research team ran a throwaway RK4 script at dt = 1/240 s in still air. Its numbers are inferences, and reproducing them should be the game's first unit test. The current model carried a PGA-average driver **248 yd against TrackMan's 282 (−12%)** and a 3-wood 225 against 249 (−10%). Irons landed at 38-44° instead of 47-50°, with apexes several yards low. In other words, drives would fall short and irons would land too flat and roll out too far. The recommended model carried the same inputs to 274 / 174 / 131 yd against 282 / 176 / 142 yd for driver, 7-iron and PW. LPGA driver, 7-iron and PW came out at 222 / 141 / 102 against 218 / 141 / 107 yd ([TrackMan targets via Top Shelf Golf](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/golf-swing-speed-chart)). LPGA apexes and landing angles fell within 2 yd and 4° of the older TrackMan sheet ([LPGA averages](https://teeituprva.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/LPGA-AVERAGES-INTERACTIVE.pdf)). The remaining bias is wedges 5-8% short and landing angles 3-4° steep. That is why the `golf.md` test target of "±3% per club" is not achievable without game-specific tuning. Use ±5% for driver through 7-iron and ±8% for wedges.

### Spin decay, timestep and wind: keep the idea, change the implementation

The planned 4%/s spin decay is fine. It is the TrackMan figure used in the Lyu simulations ([Lyu et al. 2018](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/595d/3d1f91e240cb47e153b8d0ef586b8caa67c4.pdf)). The more physical alternative, ω ← ω·e^(−0.001·V·dt) per metre travelled ([opengolfcoach](https://docs.rs/crate/opengolfcoach/0.3.0/source/src/trajectory.rs)), averages to the same value. Either way, apply it as a multiplication: a constant precomputed once, or 1 − K·V·dt, whose error at 1/240 s is negligible. Never call `exp` per step. Hold the spin axis fixed for the whole flight, as TrackMan does ([TrackMan: spin axis](https://trackman.com/blog/golf/spin-axis)). Curve then comes from axis tilt alone.

RK4 hardly cares about timestep here. In the team's script, carry changed by **under 0.3 m between dt = 1/30 s and 1/1000 s**, and most of the remaining error came from detecting the ground crossing. That has two consequences. The CPU planner can integrate flight at 1/60 s. The live game can keep cue_sim's 4 × 1/240 s substeps per tick, not for aero accuracy, but because a 70 m/s ball moves 0.29 m per substep, which keeps ground-contact detection simple.

Wind should stay a velocity offset inside the drag and lift terms, as libgolf does ([libgolf](https://gdifiore.github.io/libgolf/docs/aerodynamic_model.html)). The team's 3D check of that approach gave −20.6 / +14.5 yd for a 10 mph head- or tailwind on a tour 7-iron. Published figures are −19.2 / +14.6 ([Golfing Focus](https://golfingfocus.com/what-affects-golf-ball-distance-beware-all-the-uncontrollables/)) and −17 / +13 ([Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/weve-taken-the-guesswork-out-of-playing-in-the-wind-with-this-powerful-new-tool)). The head/tail asymmetry emerges without extra code. Two additions fix the most common store-review complaint, that "wind affected the ball the same way on a drive and a short chip" ([Golf Impact reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/golf-impact-real-golf-game/id1532689217)). First, scale wind with height using a power law (exponent about 0.15, reference height 10 m, floor of 0.3×). Second, apply zero wind to a ball rolling on the ground. Spin-axis tilt in the team's model curved the ball about 30% more than TrackMan's "about 15 yd at 10° over 200 yd" ([TrackMan](https://trackman.com/blog/golf/spin-axis)). Multiply the lateral lift component by about 0.75, or keep the stronger shaping on purpose because it reads better on a small screen.

### Compress the course with hole design, not slower balls

The hole-design research suggested scaling every club to about 0.45× real distance. Doing that by lowering launch speed would push whole flights below Re ≈ 5×10⁴, where Cd is about 0.5. Balls would balloon and drop in an unnatural way, and the TrackMan validation would no longer transfer. Keep physics in real metres. Give both golfers one casual club ladder derived from the TrackMan amateur driver row: 133 mph ball speed, 12.6°, 3,275 rpm, about 195 yd carry ([TrackMan ball data](https://hankhaney.com/app/uploads/2019/01/ball_data.pdf)). Then make the course compact the way executive courses are. Those run about half the length of a regulation course and are mostly par 3s ([CaddieHQ](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/what-is-an-executive-length-golf-course)).

## Bounce, roll and cup: one analytic model replaces the Jolt hand-off

### Why the Jolt RigidBody3D hand-off should go

Godot's Jolt documentation says nothing about determinism, and calls running physics on a separate thread "experimental" ([Godot 4.7: Using Jolt](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html)). The CPU has to try hundreds of what-if shots, and GDScript cannot cheaply clone a live Jolt world for each one. A rigid contact does not produce turf behaviour either: spin-dependent check, grass drag and spin-back all have to be written by hand anyway. The hand-off also creates two code paths and a velocity discontinuity at landing. Even Jolt's edge handling leaves "ghost bumps" at seams between separate terrain bodies ([Godot 4.7: Using Jolt](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html)), and a tile-built course has many seams.

The counter-evidence is in the repo. `docs/PROGRESS.md` records the CueSim golden-break hash as **identical on desktop and the Vivo (Adreno 642L), at 144 µs per 1/240 s substep** for 16 balls. The CPU planner there runs about 100 rollouts in 0.4 s. One golf ball against a heightfield has no pairwise collisions, so it should be far cheaper. The research team estimated 10-30 µs per substep, but that is unmeasured, and a `--bench=golf` run must confirm it. An MIT-licensed Godot golf library, OpenFairWay, also uses a fully custom headless solver with a Penner-style bounce rather than engine rigid bodies ([OpenFairWay](https://github.com/digitalhand/openfairway)). It is C#, so treat it as a reference only.

### Bounce: Penner's equations with a fixed per-surface tilt angle

Penner's model is a rigid bounce with restitution e and Coulomb friction μ, computed in a frame tilted by a crater angle. In that frame the ball either slides throughout the impact (v_x' − μ|v_y'|(1+e)), or leaves rolling at (5/7)v_x' − (2/7)rω. A critical friction value μc decides which case applies ([Penner 2002](http://waddengolfacademy.com/putting/Penner_The%20Run%20of%20a%20Golf%20Ball.pdf)). A 2023 R&A-backed study filmed more than 1,000 bounces at up to 56 m/s. It found **Penner's speed-dependent crater angle only modestly better than a rigid bounce, and a fixed tilt β (12.9° on artificial turf, 18.4° on natural turf) fitting as well** ([Biber et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.02758)). So use a fixed β per surface.

β also does gameplay work. Penner shows that on a firm green (μ = 0.25) a wedge landing with 1,000-1,200 rad/s of backspin hops forward and then **runs back 1.1-4.7 m**, while 809 rad/s roughly stops it ([Penner 2002](http://waddengolfacademy.com/putting/Penner_The%20Run%20of%20a%20Golf%20Ball.pdf)). Without a tilt, a 7-iron landing at about 55° would need roughly 1,500 rad/s to spin back. That is out of reach. The concept clip's "checks with backspin" moment therefore depends on firm-green μ and a positive β. At casual spin rates it will mostly produce a check, not a spin-back, unless the optional spin control adds a little spin. Penner's restitution fit is e = 0.510 − 0.0375v_n + 0.000903v_n² for v_n ≤ 20 m/s ([Penner 2002](http://waddengolfacademy.com/putting/Penner_The%20Run%20of%20a%20Golf%20Ball.pdf)). Switch from bouncing to rolling once bounce height falls below 5 mm. Penner found anything from 1 to 10 mm barely changes the run.

No measured restitution or friction values exist for rough, bunker sand or cart paths. The values for those surfaces in the defaults table are tuning start points, checked against Penner's first-bounce heights: about 1-3 m for drives and 0.5-1 m for mid irons.

### Roll: derive deceleration from green speed and use 5/7 of gravity on slopes

A Stimpmeter releases the ball at about 1.83-1.91 m/s ([Lee 2025](https://stephenlee.info/golf/physics/2025/11/16/rederiving-aimpoint.html)). Rolling deceleration on a green is therefore a = v0²/(2·Stimp). That gives **0.55-0.60 m/s² at Stimp 10** and 0.46-0.50 at Stimp 12, which matches an independent 0.457 m/s² at Stimp 12 ([Dillon](https://tkevindillon.substack.com/p/dies-at-the-hole)). The doc's 0.6-0.9 m/s² range corresponds to a slow green of Stimp 7-10. More important, the doc's "+ g·sin(slope)" is wrong for a rolling ball. A rolling solid sphere feels only **(5/7)·g·sin θ** along the fall line ([Lee 2025](https://stephenlee.info/golf/physics/2025/11/16/rederiving-aimpoint.html); [Dillon](https://tkevindillon.substack.com/p/dies-at-the-hole)). Using full g overstates break by 40%.

The same numbers tie the physics to green design. At a = 0.55 m/s², a stopped ball stays put on slopes up to about 7.9% grade, where (5/7)·g·sin θ equals the rolling resistance. The USGA guideline of at most about 3% around the cup ([Golf Course Industry](https://www.golfcourseindustry.com/article/green-slopes-golf)) therefore leaves balls at rest and visibly breaking. The hole-design notes suggested 6-10% ramps between tiers. Ramps that are meant to shed a short approach must be steeper than about 9%, or a stopped ball will sit on them. Fairway and rough rolling resistance is unmeasured. Start the fairway at about 1.2-2.0 m/s² (Stimp-equivalent 3-5 ft) and the rough at 3-6 m/s². Tune until a firm fairway gives a drive about 15-25 yd of roll, the gap between tour carry and total distance.

Stopping rules need care. A ball comes to rest when its speed falls below about 0.03 m/s for at least 0.25 s, so it does not stop at the top of a back-roll. It also needs the slope pull to be below the surface's static hold, set at 1.0-1.2× its rolling deceleration. Clamp deceleration so it cannot reverse the velocity within a single step, and add a 30 s hard cap. Together these remove the jitter at rest that the doc warns about, without Jolt's sleep settings.

### Cup: an offset-dependent capture speed

Holmes' 1.31 m/s free-fall limit for a centred putt ([Quintic / Sheppard 2016](https://www.quinticballroll.com/research-papers/Quintic-An-Investigation-in-to-Golf-Ball-Speed-at-Hole-Entry.pdf)) can be derived exactly from geometry. The ball is captured if v ≤ (L(b) − r_b)·√(g/2r_b), where L(b) = 2√(R² − b²) is the chord across the cup at lateral offset b. Penner's limit, which includes help from the back rim, is about 1.63 m/s ([Dillon](https://tkevindillon.substack.com/p/dies-at-the-hole)). Scaling the free-fall value by 1.24 reproduces it. The doc's single 1.3 m/s threshold is too generous off-centre: at b = 4 cm the free-fall limit falls to about 0.78 m/s. Test the cup against the ball's path segment during each substep, not just its end point, so fast balls cannot skip over it. Above the rim-assisted limit, deflect the ball by an angle proportional to b/R and keep 50-70% of its speed. That lip-out model is unsourced and needs tuning by feel.

## One thumb, one gesture: controls and camera for portrait play

### Power from pull distance, accuracy from timing, cancel always available

The best-reviewed portrait golf games, in 3D and side-on, use the same input: **press near the ball, drag back, release**. One adds a timing element on release ([West Games guide](https://west-games.com/golf-clash-controls/); [Pocket Gamer, Golf Blitz](https://www.pocketgamer.com/golf-blitz/golf-blitz-review-some-of-the-best-multiplayer-that-mobile-gaming-has-to-offer/)). The two-stroke swipe swing gets "love it or hate it" reviews ([Pocket Gamer](https://ee.pocketgamer.com/articles/084131/clap-hanz-golf-review-having-a-ball/)). Store reviews of swipe games keep returning to one complaint: swipe speed "seemed to have almost no effect", which made chips and putts hard ([Golf Dreams listing](https://game-solver.com/golf-dreams/)). That argues against the doc's "speed = power" flick. **Map power to pull distance.** Add a release-timing window for accuracy. Add an overpower band past 100% that gives extra distance but a much larger error. Using a slightly off-centre release to counter a crosswind is a skill players discover for themselves ([Playdemic FAQ](https://playdemic.helpshift.com/hc/en/6-golf-clash/faq/1285-why-did-my-shot-not-land-where-i-expected-1613730444)). Dragging back to the ball cancels the shot. That copies the purpose of Golden Tee's tap-to-cancel ([GTM help](https://gtm-help.itsgames.com/gtm-help/help/howtoplay)) and addresses a reported problem of swinging by accident while trying to pan ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/mini-golf-theme-park-99-holes-smart-phone/review)).

The timing element must not look like the genre leader's concentric rings and sweeping needle. That trade dress is strongly associated with one game. Use something such as a ring that closes around the ball, with original words for the grades. Offer a tap-to-start, tap-to-stop meter as an accessibility mode. Xbox's guideline 107 advises alternatives to drags and swipes ([Xbox XAG 107](https://devdocs.xbox.com/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107)), and side-on golf has a well-reviewed precedent for tap-tap ([148Apps](https://www.148apps.com/reviews/super-stick-golf-review)).

### Aim by landing marker, read greens by motion, show wind as drift

Aim with a draggable landing marker that shows where the ball first lands plus a few dots for the bounce. Scale its precision with difficulty, as shipped games scale guide-line length ([West Games](https://west-games.com/golf-clash-controls/)). Pick the club automatically from the marker distance and show each club's carry as a nominal number. Golden Tee warns that the listed maximum "isn't an exact landing guide" ([GTM help](https://gtm-help.itsgames.com/gtm-help/help/howtoplay)). Spin should be an optional ball-face widget, off by default and unlocked after the tutorial holes, as the market leader does ([West Games](https://west-games.com/golf-clash-controls/)).

Putting draws the most "feels bad" reviews. Close-range putting gets called "clunky" ([TouchArcade forum](https://toucharcade.com/community/threads/309558)), and one mini-golf developer exposes per-style putt-strength settings ([Mighty Coconut](https://support.mightycoconut.com/kb/article/38-three-ways-to-play-touch-to-putt-swing-to-putt-standard-play/)). So scale putt power automatically: a full pull sends the ball a little past the hole. Make putt input a straight line; break comes only from slope physics, as in Golden Tee ([GTM help](https://gtm-help.itsgames.com/gtm-help/help/howtoplay)). Show slope with a grid of dots that flow downhill, faster on steeper ground ([TrackMan green grid](https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/12894735013019-Settings-Green-Grid-Colors-In-Virtual-Golf)). Motion carries the information, so it works for colour-blind players. Show wind as an arrow and a number. On Easy, add a ghost of the predicted drift on the landing marker. Show elevation as a "plays like" distance.

### Hole up the screen, short flights, everything skippable

Portrait works for golf only if the hole runs up the screen. One forum commenter found it odd "unless the levels are very vertical" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/community/threads/413968)). The camera should be a state machine: AIM (2.5-3.5 m behind the ball, 1.2-1.6 m up, ball in the lower third), then LAUNCH_FOLLOW (looking ahead toward the landing point the solver predicts at strike), a 0.5-1 s LANDING hold, ROLL_FOLLOW, a top-down GREEN_READ, and CUP. Tap during flight to fast-forward, following Golden Tee's "Quick Shot" ([GTM help](https://gtm-help.itsgames.com/gtm-help/help/howtoplay)). Add a Reduce Motion setting that replaces follow with cuts and turns off shake and slow motion ([Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/)). Touch targets should be at least 48 dp apart by 8 dp ([Android accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)). Mirror the bottom HUD for left-handed players. Hazards need pattern as well as colour: ripples, stipple, stakes ([GAG colour rule](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)).

Pacing is the biggest risk in this section. The hole research estimated 8-12 s per shot and 6-8 minutes for nine holes, but that counts only the player's roughly 40 strokes. A CPU rival adds about 40 more, so a full nine at full speed takes 11-16 minutes. CPU shots therefore have to play at 2-4× with a skip button. The hub also needs a short mode. The hub already uses best-of-3 (Break & Bank), so offer a three-hole match-play "duel" drawn from the nine, alongside nine-hole stroke play.

## Nine executive-length holes, authored as tiles and baked to a grid

### Par 32 from one club ladder, difficulty from angles rather than length

Par means reaching the green in regulation and then two putts ([Wikipedia: Par](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par_(golf))). Define the club ladder first and derive hole lengths from it. With a casual ladder (driver about 175 m carry, hybrid 150, 7-iron 130, wedge 95 full and partial down to 10 m), par 3s come out at 75-145 m, par 4s at 230-300 m and a reachable par 5 at 360-400 m. A nine of four par 3s, four par 4s and one par 5 then totals par 32 and about 1.8-2.0 km. That sits inside the 1.6-2.3 km range of a real 9-hole executive course ([CaddieHQ](https://www.caddiehq.com/resources/what-is-an-executive-length-golf-course)). Architecture sources agree that difficulty should come from hazard placement and angles, not length, because length "penalizes weaker players" ([Golf Course Wiki](https://golfcoursewiki.substack.com/p/visualizing-risk-and-reward-on-the)). Mix the three schools: mostly strategic holes, one or two penal ones ("too many become boring"), and one or two heroic carries ([GOLF.com](https://golf.com/travel/3-schools-golf-course-architecture/)). Follow every penal hole with a forgiving "fun" hole.

| # | Par | Length (m) | Role | Key modules | Green |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 240 | Wide opener ("fun") | Straight fairway, two side bunkers | Large, 0-2% cup zones |
| 2 | 3 | 120 | Strategic | Front-left bunker pulls the aim right | Medium, one shoulder |
| 3 | 4 | 280 | Strategic, continuous reward | Dogleg with a bunker at the corner | Medium |
| 4 | 3 | 110 | Penal (drama hole) | Island green, bail-out tee box | Small, about 15 m |
| 5 | 3 | 140 | Fun, roll-out | Elevated tee, downhill funnel | Large |
| 6 | 4 | 260 | Strategic forethought | Tree cluster blocks the approach from one side | Medium |
| 7 | 5 | 380 | Heroic | Water carry to reach in two, layup lane | Large |
| 8 | 4 | 270 | Continuous hazard | Water or out-of-bounds down one side | Medium |
| 9 | 3 | 130 | Closer | Two-tier green, pin set by seed | Two plateaus, ramp steeper than 9% |

Lengths, roles and sizes in this table are a starting proposal built from the cited categories. They are to be tuned by self-play, not taken from a source.

Greens should hold cup zones at 0-3%, the USGA cupping guidance ([Golf Course Industry](https://www.golfcourseindustry.com/article/green-slopes-golf)). Shoulders can run 4-8%. The USGA and R&A treat 4% as the threshold for "significant" slope ([GolfWRX](https://www.golfwrx.com/523880/usga-will-restrict-green-reading-materials-beginning-in-2019)), so strong-slope markers should only appear above it. Size each green to the dispersion of its approach club. Real greens average about 510 m² ([USGA](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/articles/2022/06/brookline-small-greens-maintain-play.html)), roughly 25 m across. Use about 18-22 m for wedge approaches and 25-30 m for long irons. For pacing, apply a pick-up rule to both players: triple bogey is the cap. It is a casual version of the official Maximum Score format ([USGA](https://rulesworkshops.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/rules-hub/rules-modernization/major-changes/maximum-score--form-of-stroke-play.html)).

### Tiles in the editor, a heightfield at runtime, a validator in the tests

AAA golf turns scanned terrain into heightfield maps plus material masks ([EA](https://www.ea.com/news/procedural-terrain-in-ea-sports-pga-tour)). That is the right runtime form here too: a `HoleData` Resource with `PackedFloat32Array` heights on 1 m cells (0.25 m on the green), a `PackedByteArray` surface ID per cell, and tee, pin and tree positions. A 60 × 320 m corridor is about 19k samples, about 77 kB. The doc's "modular tiles" survive as the authoring layer. Tee boxes, straight and curved fairway pieces, three bunker shapes, two water bodies, three green shapes with slope presets and two tree clusters are placed in the editor. A `@tool` Bake step then writes them into the grids. The solver samples heights by triangle-barycentric interpolation, using the same diagonal as the render mesh so the ball never floats. Surface comes from a nearest-cell byte read. The same byte grid becomes the splat texture, so what the player sees is what the ball feels.

One indie procedural golf game had to add a guaranteed path after random water made courses unplayable ([ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/games/minigolf-endless-mmo)). Fairway Flick should guard against the same failure with a unit-tested validator. It checks that every forced carry has a bail-out or is within the wedge's reliable range, across all nine holes and every seed. Seeded daily variety should stay safe: vary pin (from authored cup zones), tee (two or three boxes) and wind only, all drawn from `core/Rng.gd`.

## A CPU that plans by expected strokes and misses like a real golfer

### Plan with simulation, score with an expected-strokes function

The best shot is the one that minimizes expected strokes over its own dispersion. That is not the pin, and not the target with the smallest average miss. In one analysis it was 4.6 yd away from a pin tucked beside a bunker, worth 0.06 strokes per shot ([Summers](https://neilsummers.github.io/golf/target-selection)). Broadie's PGA TOUR table of strokes to hole out, by distance and lie, is the standard function to minimize ([Broadie 2012](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4996/assessing_golfer_performance.full.pdf)). Its shape transfers to this game: concave in distance, with fixed lie penalties of about +0.2 for rough, +0.3-0.5 for sand and +0.6-0.9 for recovery. Its yardages do not transfer to executive holes. Refit E(distance, lie) offline by running the strongest CPU tier on the nine holes.

The planner runs on a `WorkerThreadPool` task over pure data. First, a club × power × spin table is built at hole load from the real solver. Next, about 126 nominal candidates (3 clubs × 3 powers × 7 aims × 2 spins) are pre-scored from that table, toward target zones: pin, centre of the green, a "fat side" point, a layup and the fairway corner. The top 10-20 then go through the real solver, now at 1/60 s. Finally, 64-128 noise samples per candidate are scored in landing space. Every candidate uses the same noise vectors (common random numbers), so differences between candidates come from the candidates and not the dice. For putts, sweep 21 aims × 5 speeds through a cheap 2D roll, refine the best three, and score with Broadie's analytic putting model. That model has direction SD 1.46°, distance SD 5.7% and aims 0.5 yd past. The team's reimplementation reproduced Broadie's 50% make distance of 8 ft and GOLF.com's tour make rates within a few points ([Broadie 2012](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4996/assessing_golfer_performance.full.pdf); [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/instruction/putting/pga-tour-putting-make-percentages-distance/)). The in-repo benchmark of about 100 rollouts in 0.4 s on the test phone suggests the whole plan fits in 1 s. Show a fixed 1.0-1.8 s "thinking" sweep across the real candidates, labelled with an intent ("lay up", "go for it"), so fast and slow phones feel identical.

### Tiers as vectors of real-golf error, with no rubber-banding

Broadie's Golfmetrics data separates skill groups along independent axes ([Broadie 2008](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/2851/broadie_wscg_v_200802.pdf)). Each tier should copy a group. The sigmas below are the research team's Monte Carlo conversion of Broadie's median miss distances into Gaussian standard deviations.

| CPU tier (proxy group) | Tee direction SD | 100-150 yd lateral / distance σ (% of shot) | 20-60 yd distance:direction ratio | Putting σθ / σd | Disaster rate per non-putt shot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy (Am3, 98-120 scorers) | 8.1° | 11.8% / 17.7% | 3.1 | about 2.0° / 0.20 | about 15% |
| Medium (Am2, 84-97) | 6.4° | 8.2% / 12.3% | 3.3 | about 1.9° / 0.13 | about 8% |
| Hard (Am1, 70-83) | 5.4° | 5.9% / 8.9% | 3.2 | about 1.6° / 0.11 | about 4% |
| Pro, unlockable (Pro1) | 4.0° | 3.7% / 5.5% | 1.5 | 1.46° / 0.057 | about 0.3% |

Three details keep a CPU from looking robotic. Distance error should exceed direction error. Weaker tiers should miss short: from 160 yd, amateurs finish 40-100 ft short far more often than long ([Golf Insider UK](https://golfinsideruk.com/approach-play-stats/)). And there should be a fat tail of mishits, because about 4% of an Am1's swings cost about 70% of the strokes lost to scratch ([Broadie 2008](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/2851/broadie_wscg_v_200802.pdf)). Decision quality is its own axis: Broadie notes awful shots can come from "a strategy that is too risky". Weak tiers should therefore choose by softmax over expected strokes, and plan as if they were 30-50% straighter than they are. Strong tiers should take the best option and aim at the fat side.

Practitioners report that players notice rubber-banding and resent it in both directions ([wayline.io](https://www.wayline.io/blog/dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-beyond-rubberbanding)). Use fixed profiles, the same physics and wind, and execution error drawn after the plan is fixed. Derive seeds from (match seed, hole, stroke), with separate streams for planning noise and execution noise. Any adaptation should happen between matches: suggest the next tier, or offer handicap strokes at the 100% allowance used in singles match play ([Golf Monthly](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/what-are-handicap-allowances-in-singles-golf)). Adjacent real skill groups differ by 8-13 strokes over 18 holes ([Broadie 2008](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/2851/broadie_wscg_v_200802.pdf)), so expect about 4-6 per nine. Target player win rates of roughly 70 / 50 / 30% for Easy / Medium / Hard. Those targets are a design choice with no published benchmark. Tune each tier's single sigma multiplier by headless self-play with the `ai-opponent` skill. Even the Hard CPU must miss putts, since tour pros make only about 41% from 10 ft ([GOLF.com](https://golf.com/instruction/putting/pga-tour-putting-make-percentages-distance/)).

## Holding 60 fps: a Godot recipe built around the Mobile renderer

Stay on the Mobile (Vulkan) renderer, which the project already uses. In 4.7 it "matches or even outperforms" Compatibility in a number of benchmarks ([Godot 4.7 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)). Use one terrain shader driven by the surface texture. Mowing stripes, fringe and grass fuzz are maths in the shader, with no geometry. Draw trees with MultiMesh (2-6 draw calls per hole). Under the ball, project a blob shadow onto the HoleData height, which also helps the player judge the ball's height in flight. Add one directional-light shadow only on the High tier. Use a procedural sky with fog. Water should be unlit and must not read the screen or depth texture. An Arm investigation showed that on tile-based GPUs, writing MSAA and resolve attachments back to memory can cut a trivial scene to 50 fps on a Pixel 8 Pro. A 4.4 fix made the same scene V-sync locked ([Arm Community](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/optimizing-3d-scenes-in-godot-on-arm-gpus)). So set MSAA 2x once in project settings and never change it mid-play, because changing MSAA level causes pipeline-compilation stutter ([Godot 4.7: pipeline compilations](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/performance/pipeline_compilations.html)). Use a 3D render scale of 0.75-0.8 with UI at native resolution. Render every material for one hidden frame during the hole transition so pipelines compile up front. Draw the trail as a fixed strip mesh positioned in the vertex shader, with no per-frame mesh rebuilds. Working budgets are about 100 draw calls and 150-250k triangles. Those are unsourced targets to verify with Perfetto.

Interpolation needs one rule. The ball's transform is set only in `_physics_process`, after four substeps, so it keeps physics interpolation ON. The camera rig, trail, aim marker and other 3D markers move in `_process`, so they are `top_level` with interpolation OFF and follow `get_global_transform_interpolated()`. That is the camera pattern Godot prescribes ([Godot 4.7: advanced interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/interpolation/advanced_physics_interpolation.html)). It also respects the CLAUDE.md rule from the 2026-11-11 smearing incident, as long as every drop or hole change calls `reset_physics_interpolation()`. Damp the camera with 1 − e^(−k·dt). Keep it above the terrain by sampling HoleData rather than using SpringArm3D, which would need colliders. Set the camera's `keep_aspect` to KEEP_WIDTH with a 55-65° horizontal FOV so the portrait view is not a sliver. This setting comes from the implementation notes and still needs checking against the 4.7 class reference.

Ground contact uses a swept test. Each substep evaluates clearance at both ends and at every cell boundary the ball crosses, then bisects 6-8 times on a sign change. That stops a 70 m/s ball tunnelling through a ridge, with no CCD. Trees are analytic cylinders and spheres. One project-level catch: the 4.5+ shader baker is **not supported for exports run with `--headless`** ([Godot 4.7: pipeline compilations](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/performance/pipeline_compilations.html)). The release export command in CLAUDE.md therefore skips it without warning. For the low-end 30 fps floor, drop render scale to 0.6 and turn off MSAA, shadows and half the trees. Solver cost does not change, because it is independent of the render rate.

## Recommended defaults, assumption changes and build order

### Where the existing assumptions should change

| Current assumption (golf.md / rules) | Change to | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cd 0.27, rising to 0.32 above S = 0.1 | Cd = 0.225 + 0.275 × ramp(Re), a linear blend from 1 at ≤5×10⁴ to 0 at ≥10⁵, + 0.18·(1 − e^(−3S²)). Piecewise-linear in Re, spin term tabulated | Full shots land inside the drag crisis. The step model makes drives 10-12% short |
| Cl = 0.54·S^0.4, clamped at 0.25 | Cl = 1.99S − 3.255S² for S ≤ 0.25, then 0.2941 + 0.3625(S − 0.25), no clamp | The clamp binds on irons and flattens them. The power law has no primary source and needs `pow` |
| Spin decay about 4%/s | Keep, applied as a precomputed per-step multiplier (or 1 − 0.001·V·dt) | `exp` per step risks cross-device differences |
| RK4 at 240 Hz | Live: 4 × 1/240 s substeps (for contact). CPU planner: 1/60 s flight, 1/120 s roll | Carry is stable to 0.3 m from 1/30 to 1/1000 s |
| Landing hands off to Jolt RigidBody3D with CCD | Custom bounce, roll, cup and tree solver end to end. No Jolt bodies for gameplay | Determinism, cheap rollouts, one code path. cue_sim already proves the approach on device |
| Green decel 0.6-0.9 m/s² + g·sin(slope) | a = v0²/(2·Stimp) with v0 = 1.83 m/s (default Stimp 10, so 0.55-0.6), + **(5/7)**·g·sin θ | Full g overstates break by 40% |
| Cup: drops if speed is under about 1.3 m/s | v_ff(b) = (2√(R² − b²) − r_b)·√(g/2r_b), rim assist ×1.24 (1.63 m/s centred), segment test | Off-centre capture is much harder |
| Wind as a constant offset | Keep the offset, add a height power law (α ≈ 0.15, floor 0.3), zero on the ground | Chips should drift less than drives |
| Carry within ±3% of the table | ±5% driver to 7-iron, ±8% wedges, plus apex, landing angle and wind tests | ±3% is not achievable with literature models |
| Swipe-back-and-flick, speed = power | Pull-distance power + release timing + overpower band. Flick optional | Swipe-speed power is the top consistency complaint |
| Nine holes from modular tiles | Tiles at authoring time, baked to heightfield and surface grids, plus a playability validator | The solver needs a sampled grid, not meshes |
| `--headless --export-release` | Non-headless release export (xvfb on CI), or accept a slower first launch | The shader baker skips headless exports |

## Conclusion

The two flagged assumptions both trade correctness for convenience that turns out not to be there. The stepped Cd and clamped Cl save no computation over the literature fits. They also need `pow`, a determinism hazard the polynomial Cl removes. The Jolt hand-off saves no code, because turf behaviour has to be written by hand anyway, and it would cost the bit-exactness that makes the CPU, replays and golden tests possible. Once the solver is one pure class, three things that looked separate depend on it. The CPU's expected-strokes planner is just more calls to that class. The mini-golf scope cut is just steps 3, 4 and 7. And green design becomes a calculation: the 0.55 m/s² rolling resistance sets the roughly 8% slope a ball can rest on, so cup slopes and tier ramps can be set from the physics.

The weakest links are now empirical rather than theoretical. No measured turf constants exist for rough, sand or paths. GDScript solver cost on mid-range Android has to be benchmarked. The 70/50/30 win-rate targets and the pacing estimates are design choices. Build order puts the carry test, the device benchmark and self-play early so that these get measured before the art and the nine holes are built on top of them.
