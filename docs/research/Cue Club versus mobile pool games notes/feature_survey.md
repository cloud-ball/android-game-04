# Feature survey: what well-reviewed mobile pool games offer, and what players praise or complain about

Scope note: this survey covers app-store listings (App Store pages were readable; Google Play pages returned no body to the fetch tool), press reviews (iMore, TouchArcade via search snippets, Pocket Gamer via search snippets), and a help-centre article for a browser/mobile casual 8-ball game. Product names appear only as source labels. Everything below is written as a genre convention to benchmark against; nothing here is a recommendation to copy a specific game's name, art, UI layout or music. Reddit threads could not be reached in this pass (see Gaps under each question).

Cue Club baseline used for comparison (from the brief and docs/games/pool.md): portrait, fixed near-top-down camera, point-to-aim plus left fine-aim strip, right pull-down power bar, spin panel with presets, solver guideline whose object-ball ray length depends on difficulty, Casual 8 (only the 8 is called, ball in hand anywhere after every foul), a single rack vs CPU on Easy/Medium/Hard, coins, daily challenges, animated how-to cards, no online play.

## 1. Which features are near-universal in highly rated mobile pool games, and which are differentiators?

### Takeaway
Single-player vs CPU with 3 or more difficulty levels, more than one rule set (8-ball plus 9-ball at minimum, often UK 8-ball and snooker), a rules-free practice mode, spin control, and cue/table cosmetics show up in almost every well-rated offline pool app. Cue Club has the CPU and spin but lacks a practice/free-play mode, a second rule set, stats and any multi-frame format, which are the most visible gaps. Differentiators are deeper: many tables and table shapes, league/knockout formats, player profiles with ranks, shot replay in practice, arcade score-attack twists, and daily or weekly preset-break puzzles.

It is free with ads and a £3.99 "Remove Ads" purchase. — [Real Pool 3D, App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/real-pool-3d/id1156081906)

### Inferences
- Near-universal in offline-first pool apps: vs-CPU with at least 3 levels; practice/free play with no rules; spin; at least 8-ball plus 9-ball; cue and cloth cosmetics. Cue Club has 2 of these 5 (CPU levels, spin). It has no practice/free-play table and only one rule set.
- Common but not universal: pass-and-play on one device, UK 8-ball and snooker, stats, achievements, tournament/league ladders against a set of CPU characters.
- Differentiators worth weighing for an offline hub: (a) a "free table" practice with cue-ball reposition and "replay last shot", which is cheap given Cue Club's deterministic solver (re-run from a saved state); (b) daily preset-break puzzles, which fit Cue Club's existing daily challenges and seeded Rng; (c) a short ladder of named CPU characters across difficulty instead of a bare Easy/Medium/Hard menu; (d) a difficulty-multiplied score so Hard pays more coins.
- Pass-and-play costs almost nothing in a turn-based game and is listed by the highest-rated 3D app; it fits a no-online hub.

### Gaps
- Google Play listing bodies (feature lists, review text, rating distributions) did not load, so Android-specific star breakdowns are missing.
- No source gave a share-of-apps count (for example, "X of top 20 have 9-ball"); "near-universal" is judged from the listings above, not a census.

## 2. Controls: aiming, fine aim, power, spin, ball in hand, pocket calling, and what feels good or bad

### Takeaway
The common pattern is drag-to-rotate the cue (often anywhere on screen, so the finger does not hide the ball) plus a separate fine-tune control, a separate power control (bar or pull-back), and a cue-ball spin inset that opens a contact-point picker. The single most repeated control complaint is the aim moving when the finger lifts or when the player starts the power stroke, which is exactly the risk in Cue Club's point-to-aim plus separate power bar.

### Cited Findings
- Aim in a large online 8-ball game: "tap and drag on the table in front of the cue tip to move the cue quickly", then "drag from the cue's handle for precise adjustments"; spin is set by tapping a cue-ball button in the top right and picking a contact point ("near the bottom adds backspin", near the top adds topspin, sides add side). — [iMore tips, updated May 2016](https://www.imore.com/8-ball-pool-6-tips-and-hints-beginners) (older than the 2018 preference; kept because it describes the dominant online convention)
- Casual web 8-ball: hold and pull back; "The exact position of the cursor doesn't matter, the direction in relation to the ball does"; "The further you pull back, the faster it goes". Spin is chosen by contact point: centre normal, slightly below centre stun, above follow, below draw, sides curve. Ball in hand: drag the cue ball while it shows a circle; after an opponent's foul always; on the break only within a limited area. Pocket calling for the 8 is an optional rule ("if that rule is chosen"). — [Arkadium help centre](https://support.arkadium.com/en/support/solutions/articles/44002181779--8-ball-pool-how-to-play-settings-daily-challenge-multiplayer-tips-scoring)
- Arcade pool: "drag anywhere on the screen to rotate the cue", tap arrows to pick which side the cue comes from, and flick to shoot, with power from flick strength; praised as intuitive and physics "respond accurately to flick power". — [iMore review, May 2018](https://imore.com/pocket-run-pool-review-arcade-pool-makes-comeback)
- Same game, criticism: "The aim trajectory can move slightly after you lift your finger, which makes difficult shots harder." — [iMore review, May 2018](https://imore.com/pocket-run-pool-review-arcade-pool-makes-comeback). A TouchArcade review (search snippet) says aiming by dragging left and right works well but wished "tiny movements were easier to make". — [TouchArcade (search snippet)](https://toucharcade.com/?p=312795)
- A user found the flick-power mechanic unreliable: flicking up exits the game on a gesture-navigation iPhone and "half the time fails to give any power at all" (search summary of App Store review). — [Pocket Run Pool, App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id949982914)
- An early Pocket Gamer iPhone pool review flagged that lifting the finger after lining up a shot can nudge the aiming line (search snippet). — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/midnight-pool-iphone/review/)
- Offline 8-ball app reviews: "when I'm pulling the stick back on the power button it moves your shot and you have to start over" (2023); "When you try to shoot, the aim screws up" (2025). — [8 Ball Billiards - Offline, App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/8-ball-billiards-offline/id1559177737)
- Android offline app: one reviewer says "the stick sometimes shoots while the player is still aiming"; another wishes the aiming line were "bigger or longer"; another says aim "snaps to angles instead of allowing infinite freedom" (search summaries of Play reviews). — [Pool Billiards offline, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.andregal.android.poolbilliard&hl=en_GB); [Pool Billiards Pro, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.forthblue.pool&hl=en_US)
- Spin that has no visible effect is a 1-star-level complaint: "the cue ball spin mechanics are completely missing. You can apply spin to your shots but it has ZERO effect." — [Real Pool 3D, App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/real-pool-3d/id1156081906)
- Some apps offer a choice of shooting by "finger swipe or power meter". — [Killer Pool, TouchArcade listing](https://toucharcade.com/games/killer-pool)
- Shot timers draw complaints when players vs a CPU or in casual play: "run out of time for my shots often enough to be an issue"; "There is no time to let you know your time is up." — [8 Ball Billiards - Offline, App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/8-ball-billiards-offline/id1559177737)

### Inferences
- Cue Club's control set (coarse aim + separate fine-aim strip + separate power bar + spin panel with presets) matches the genre's mainstream. The pull-down bar is a good choice: reviews of flick power report misfires and system-gesture conflicts, which a bar avoids.
- The highest-value check for Cue Club is aim stability: (a) the aim must not change on finger lift (the pool.md spec calls for ignoring the last 50–80 ms before lift; confirm point-to-aim still does this), and (b) touching the power bar must never move the aim. Both are named in reviews from 2018 to 2025.
- "Aim snaps to angles" is a complaint. Cue Club's light snap on Easy/Medium should be subtle and visible, or toggleable, so it does not read as lost freedom.
- Spin must be visibly effective, for example a cue-ball path in the guideline that bends with spin, or a post-shot "DRAW SHOT" label, since "spin does nothing" is a recurring 1-star claim even when spin works.
- Casual conventions favour ball in hand with a visible ring and dragging; break ball in hand is behind the head string in one game. Cue Club's "anywhere after every foul" is the more forgiving end of the range. Optional 8-ball calling exists as a setting elsewhere; Cue Club fixes it on.
- No shot timer vs CPU (presumably Cue Club's default) avoids a documented complaint.

### Gaps
- No review compared drag-anywhere aim with point-at-finger aim directly; the evidence is complaint patterns, not A/B data.
- No source detailed how leading apps implement the fine-aim widget beyond "drag the cue handle"; numeric sensitivity values were not found.
- No player feedback found specifically on presets such as Follow/Draw buttons vs a free contact-point picker.

## 3. Guidelines: length, object-ball and cue-ball paths, difficulty scaling, aim-assist complaints

### Takeaway
Guideline length is the genre's main assist dial. Online games tie it to cue upgrades; simulation apps tie it to selectable handicap levels; casual games show short object-ball and cue-ball deflection stubs. Players complain both ways: guidelines too short on beginner settings, and paid long guidelines as unfair. Cue Club's difficulty-scaled object-ball ray matches the offline convention.

### Cited Findings
- In the dominant online 8-ball game, a cue's "Aim" stat is the length of the guideline, and upgraded cues "extend aiming guides"; once lined up, "short aiming lines show the direction the target ball will roll". — [iMore tips, 2016](https://www.imore.com/8-ball-pool-6-tips-and-hints-beginners); [bestforandroid tips](https://bestforandroid.com/8-ball-pool-tips-tricks/)
- The same iMore piece recommends players hold a straight edge (a Post-it) against the screen to extend the short guideline to the pocket, which shows how much short guidelines constrain players. — [iMore tips, 2016](https://www.imore.com/8-ball-pool-6-tips-and-hints-beginners)
- Third-party "long line" aim hacks are widely promoted, showing demand for longer guidelines (the hack sites themselves are unreliable and warn of bans). — [search results summary; e.g. joytify](https://www.joytify.com/blog/en-ph/8-ball-poll-mod-apk/)
- A simulation app offers "5 handicap levels with aiming and ball-guide mark-ups", decoupled from CPU difficulty. — [Pro Pool 2026, App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/pro-pool-2024/id556429076)
- Arcade pool shows a white cue-ball path line and "a dotted circle marks where the cue ball should end up" (ghost-ball style). — [iMore review, 2018](https://imore.com/pocket-run-pool-review-arcade-pool-makes-comeback)
- Android players praise "useful aiming guide lines" and also ask for the line to be "bigger or longer" (search summaries of Play reviews). — [Pool Billiards offline, AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/pool-billiards-offline/com.andregal.android.poolbilliard); [Pool Billiards Pro, Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.forthblue.pool&hl=en_US)
- In online play, opponents with suspiciously perfect long shots are blamed on "aim bots and impossible precision shots" (aggregated review summary; low reliability). — [askapp.top](https://askapp.top/games/8-ball-pool); [Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/8ballpool.com?page=3)

### Inferences
- Cue Club's approach (aim line to ghost ball, object-ball ray length by difficulty, cue-ball ray) is in line with the genre. Two gaps compared with simulation apps: (a) assist level is tied to CPU difficulty, whereas some apps let players pick aim assist separately from opponent strength; (b) no "guideline off" pro option.
- Because there is no online play, Cue Club has no fairness reason to keep guidelines short. A longer guideline on Easy costs nothing competitively and answers the "make the line longer" request. The coin economy should not gate guideline length; cue-stat paywalls are tied to pay-to-win perceptions in online games.
- Showing the cue-ball path after contact, including spin, helps with the "spin does nothing" complaint.

### Gaps
- No source gave numeric guideline lengths (in ball diameters or cm) for any app.
- No reliable review set quantified how often "aim assist too strong" appears versus "too weak" in offline apps.

## 4. Camera: top-down vs 3D, stroke and follow cameras, phone preferences

### Takeaway
Well-featured simulation apps offer several cameras (3D behind-the-cue, overhead/top-down, rail-level) and let players choose a post-shot camera. On small screens, reviewers prefer the overhead view for aiming and criticise behind-the-cue 3D views that hide the table. Cue Club's fixed near-top-down view matches what players use for aiming. Its missing piece compared with the genre is an optional post-release flourish or a player-selectable view, not a 3D aiming camera.

### Cited Findings
- A simulation app offers 3D, Top Cushion and Overhead views and lets each profile set its preferred post-shot camera. — [Pro Pool 2026, App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/pro-pool-2024/id556429076)
- Other apps advertise top-down plus first-person views, or Free View plus First Person View. — [Pool Table Challenge, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6444575964); [Pool Break Lite, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id422185019) (search summaries)
- A reviewer of a 3D app says they mostly play from a top-down view (search summary). — [Real Pool 3D, App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/real-pool-3d/id1156081906)
- On a handheld screen, a reviewer said a behind-the-shot console pool game's camera "could benefit from offering a wider view of the table", with dark corners that hinder aiming "especially on the Switch's portable screen"; another found that camera harder than an overhead view (search summaries; Switch handheld, not phone). — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/pure_pool); [Operation Sports](https://operationsports.com/pure-pool-review-a-perfect-nintendo-switch-game)
- The mass-market online 8-ball convention and the arcade game are both played from a fixed overhead 2D/near-2D view. — [iMore tips](https://www.imore.com/8-ball-pool-6-tips-and-hints-beginners); [iMore review](https://imore.com/pocket-run-pool-review-arcade-pool-makes-comeback)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports Cue Club's fixed portrait top-down aiming camera. A "stroke cam" or low swoop after release (already described as the concept-clip moment in pool.md) is the 3D-genre flourish players like, but it should be optional or brief and should return to overhead before the next aim.
- A settings toggle (overhead only / cinematic after shot) mirrors the per-profile post-shot camera convention and doubles as the reduce-motion option.

### Gaps
- No phone-specific survey or review quantifying top-down vs 3D preference was found; the Switch-handheld evidence is a proxy.
- No sources on follow-camera behaviour (tracking the cue ball vs centroid) in mobile pool games.

## 5. Session design vs CPU: match length, best-of, difficulty, rewards

### Takeaway
Offline pool apps usually layer a short Quick Play frame under longer structures: leagues (7 rounds in one app), knockout tournaments (4 rounds), ranked ladders of named CPU opponents (25 across 5 tiers in one app), or score-attack runs and daily/weekly preset puzzles. Rewards are coins scaled by difficulty, unlockable cues/cloths, achievements and stats. Cue Club's single rack with three levels sits at the thinnest end of this range.

### Cited Findings
- Quick Play, a 7-round League and a 4-round knockout Tournament; 25 computer opponents across 5 difficulty levels; ranks Rookie to Legend that can go down; profile statistics; 20+ achievements. — [Pro Pool 2026, App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/pro-pool-2024/id556429076)
- 3 difficulty levels and a practice mode in a 4.6-star app. — [Real Pool 3D, App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/real-pool-3d/id1156081906)
- Difficulty multiplies score (Easy ×1, Medium ×1.5, Hard ×2); daily challenge with one try (three for subscribers). — [Arkadium help centre](https://support.arkadium.com/en/support/solutions/articles/44002181779--8-ball-pool-how-to-play-settings-daily-challenge-multiplayer-tips-scoring)
- A daily one-attempt tournament on a preset break and a retryable weekly preset puzzle; cosmetic cues/colour schemes unlocked by winning a harder mode. — [iMore review, 2018](https://imore.com/pocket-run-pool-review-arcade-pool-makes-comeback); [Pocket Run Pool, App Store CA](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/pocket-run-pool/id949982914)
- A user asked for "some kind of daily cash amount so that players that wager too much ... get a chance to get back on their feet". — [Pocket Run Pool, App Store CA](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/pocket-run-pool/id949982914)
- Offline coin/level/tournament loops with daily spins and chests imitate online meta-progression. — [8 Ball Billiards - Offline, App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/8-ball-billiards-offline/id1559177737); [iMore tips](https://www.imore.com/8-ball-pool-6-tips-and-hints-beginners)

### Inferences
- Low-cost additions that fit Cue Club's offline hub: an immediate "Rematch" (same difficulty, alternate break), optional "race to 2/3" frames, and a short CPU ladder (for example 5 to 8 named opponents rising in noise/planning budget) that gives a reason to return without online play.
- Scale coin rewards by difficulty and by clean play (no-foul, multi-ball pots), following the casual scoring convention.
- If coins can be spent on anything wagered, include a daily floor so players cannot run out.
- Stats per game (win rate by difficulty, pot %, longest run, break-and-runs) are listed by the stronger apps and are cheap with a SaveService namespace.

### Gaps
- No source gave typical session length in minutes for a mobile 8-ball frame vs CPU.
- No data on whether best-of formats raise retention compared with single racks in offline pool.

## 6. Most common 1-star / 5-star themes (physics, CPU fairness, ads, controls, performance)

### Takeaway
Across listings and reviews, 5-star themes are realistic or responsive physics, clean readable visuals and sounds, intuitive controls, and offline play that works with no sign-in. 1-star themes are excessive or badly timed ads, CPUs that feel rigged ("never misses", "steals shots", unfair first-player advantage), aim that shifts during lift or power input, spin or pocket physics that look wrong, crashes and load failures, and offline games that still need a connection. Cue Club's offline, CPU-only design avoids the online-cheating and connection themes; CPU fairness, aim stability and spin feedback are the themes it must still guard against.

- CPU fairness is about perception as much as strength. Cue Club's noise-based CPU should miss in believable ways (thin cuts and long pots more than straight-ins), avoid improbable bank or combination wins on Easy/Medium, and never benefit from rule asymmetry. A visible "CPU fouled → ball in hand" explanation and the same rules shown to both sides counter the "AI steals a shot" impression. The Hard CPU "never missing" a hard shot is a complaint even from players who like the game.
- Safety play by the CPU should leave the player in real trouble; leaving an easy shot after a safety reads as unrealistic.
- Pocket rattles and jaw bounces must look plausible, because "bounces back and forth at the pockets" is a cited complaint. Cue Club's pocket-geometry model is the relevant system.
- Truly offline with no sign-in, fast cold start and no mid-turn ads directly answer the most repeated 1-star themes. If the hub ever shows ads, they belong between frames, never inside a timed challenge.
